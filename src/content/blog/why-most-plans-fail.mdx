Most plans fail not because you lack discipline, but because they're built without your real life in mind. They assume steady schedules, unlimited energy, and a version of you who never has an off week.

## The all-or-nothing trap

When a plan depends on perfect execution, the first missed day feels like failure — so you abandon the whole thing. The problem was never your commitment. It was a plan with no room for being human.

## What works instead

A durable approach is built on flexibility, not rigidity. Instead of asking "did I follow the plan?", start asking "did I move in the right direction?" That shift takes practice, but it changes everything.

The goal isn't a flawless system. It's a way of working that doesn't require constant willpower to keep alive.
